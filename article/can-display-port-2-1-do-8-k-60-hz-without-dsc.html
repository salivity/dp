Can DisplayPort 2.1 Do 8K 60Hz Without DSC?

DisplayPort 2.1 can achieve an 8K resolution at 60 Hz without relying on Display Stream Compression (DSC). Thanks to the introduction of Ultra-High Bit Rate (UHBR) transmission modes, the standard provides sufficient raw bandwidth to transmit uncompressed 8K video signals at standard refresh rates. However, whether a setup achieves this depends on the specific UHBR tier implemented by the source device, monitor, and interconnect cable.

Bandwidth Demands of Uncompressed 8K at 60 Hz

Displaying an 8K resolution (7680 × 4320 pixels) at a 60 Hz refresh rate generates a massive amount of data. The exact bandwidth requirement depends on the color depth and pixel format:

  • 8-bit Color (SDR, 4:4:4 / RGB): Requires approximately 49.65 Gbps of effective data bandwidth using VESA CVT-RB2 (Reduced Blanking) timing.
  • 10-bit Color (HDR, 4:4:4 / RGB): Requires approximately 62.06 Gbps of effective data bandwidth using VESA CVT-RB2 timing.

Previous standards, such as DisplayPort 1.4a, capped effective data throughput at 25.92 Gbps, making DSC mandatory for running 8K at 60 Hz.

DisplayPort 2.1 Bandwidth Profiles

DisplayPort 2.1 uses a 128b/132b line coding scheme, delivering an efficiency rate of approximately 96.7%. The standard defines three distinct transmission speed tiers across a full 4-lane connection:

  • UHBR 10: 40 Gbps raw link rate; 38.69 Gbps effective bandwidth.
  • UHBR 13.5: 54 Gbps raw link rate; 52.22 Gbps effective bandwidth.
  • UHBR 20: 80 Gbps raw link rate; 77.37 Gbps effective bandwidth.

Feasibility Without DSC

Because DSC is visually lossless but still technically compression, users seeking completely native pixel transmission must look at the specific tier in use:

  • Under UHBR 20: DisplayPort 2.1 easily handles uncompressed 8K at 60 Hz with full 10-bit color (HDR) and 4:4:4 chroma, utilizing roughly 62 Gbps of the available 77.37 Gbps payload capacity.
  • Under UHBR 13.5: DisplayPort 2.1 can handle uncompressed 8K at 60 Hz using 8-bit color (SDR), as the ~49.65 Gbps requirement fits inside the 52.22 Gbps effective limit. 10-bit HDR at this tier requires either DSC or 4:2:2/4:2:0 chroma subsampling.
  • Under UHBR 10: Uncompressed 8K at 60 Hz is not possible in standard RGB mode, requiring DSC to reduce the data stream below the 38.69 Gbps ceiling.

Practical Requirements

To run uncompressed 8K at 60 Hz without compression artifacts or latency, every link in the display chain must support high-tier DisplayPort 2.1 capabilities:

  1. Source: A graphics card capable of driving UHBR 13.5 (for 8-bit) or UHBR 20 (for 10-bit HDR).
  2. Display: A monitor or display controller with receiver hardware rated for UHBR 13.5 or UHBR 20.
  3. Cabling: A VESA-certified DP80 cable capable of sustaining full 80 Gbps speeds over passive or active connections. Standard DP40 cables only guarantee speeds up to UHBR 10.
