How LFC Works Below DisplayPort VRR Range

Low Framerate Compensation (LFC) is a display technology that prevents stuttering and screen tearing when a video game's framerate drops below the minimum refresh rate supported by a Variable Refresh Rate (VRR) monitor. Operating over DisplayPort interfaces through protocols like AMD FreeSync and NVIDIA G-SYNC Compatible, LFC dynamically duplicates rendered frames to keep the monitor’s refresh rate within its active VRR window, ensuring smooth gameplay even under heavy rendering loads.

The VRR Window Problem

DisplayPort Variable Refresh Rate relies on the VESA Adaptive-Sync standard to dynamically synchronize the monitor's vertical refresh rate to the GPU’s output framerate. However, all VRR monitors feature a specific operating range, commonly 48 Hz to 144 Hz or 48 Hz to 240 Hz.

When a game’s output falls below the bottom threshold (for example, dropping to 35 FPS on a 48–144 Hz panel), the monitor can no longer slow its refresh cycle down to match the GPU. Without an intervention mechanism, the monitor would be forced to drop out of VRR mode, reverting to fixed refresh behavior that triggers noticeable visual stutter or screen tearing.

How LFC Operates: Frame Multiplication

LFC solves this issue by mathematically multiplying the framerate at the GPU driver level before sending the signal across the DisplayPort link:

  1. Threshold Detection: The graphics driver continuously monitors frame presentation times. As soon as the rendering rate dips below the monitor’s lower VRR limit, the driver engages LFC algorithms.
  2. Frame Duplication: The GPU repeats each rendered frame multiple times. If a game renders at 30 FPS on a display with a 48 Hz minimum limit, the GPU sends each frame twice, creating an effective transmission rate of 60 Hz.
  3. Synchronized Display: Because 60 Hz falls comfortably within the monitor's 48–144 Hz VRR range, the DisplayPort Adaptive-Sync connection remains active. The display refreshes twice for each single game frame, matching the multiplied rate perfectly without dropping synchronization.

If framerates collapse even further—such as down to 20 FPS—the driver can increase the multiplier, displaying each frame three times to hit a stable 60 Hz, or four times to hit 80 Hz, dynamically adjusting the multiplier based on real-time performance.

Hardware Requirements for LFC

LFC does not require specialized hardware inside the display scaler, but it does require a specific refresh rate ratio. For LFC to function effectively, the display’s maximum refresh rate must typically be at least double its minimum refresh rate (a 2:1 ratio or greater).

A display with a range of 48 Hz to 144 Hz easily supports LFC because 144 is three times 48. Conversely, a budget monitor with an Adaptive-Sync range of 40 Hz to 60 Hz lacks sufficient headroom; doubling a 35 FPS signal would require 70 Hz, exceeding the monitor's maximum capability and making LFC mathematically impossible.

Transition Smoothness

The primary technical challenge of LFC over DisplayPort is managing the boundary transition where framerates hover directly around the minimum threshold (e.g., fluctuating between 46 FPS and 50 FPS). Modern GPU drivers utilize adaptive hysteresis algorithms to prevent rapid toggling between standard VRR and LFC modes. By smoothly ramping the refresh multiplier up and down, the system prevents sudden luminance shifts and frame pacing spikes, maintaining consistent visual fluidity.
