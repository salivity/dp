DisplayPort Handling of Sudden Monitor Power Loss

When a connected monitor experiences a sudden power cutoff, the DisplayPort (DP) protocol relies on hardware-level signaling and software-level handshakes to immediately update the display topology. The protocol drops the Hot Plug Detect (HPD) signal, halts main-link video transmission, invalidates DisplayPort Configuration Data (DPCD), and triggers the host operating system to reallocate desktop workspace and active windows to remaining displays.

The Role of Hot Plug Detect (HPD)

The primary mechanism for detecting a sudden power loss is the physical Hot Plug Detect (HPD) pin (Pin 18 on a standard DisplayPort connector). Under normal operation, the monitor (the Sink) supplies a steady +3.3V signal to the source (the GPU).

When power is severed unexpectedly:

  • The monitor's internal power rail collapses, causing the voltage on the HPD line to drop to 0V (logical low).
  • According to the VESA DisplayPort standard, a low signal lasting longer than 2 milliseconds (specifically 2ms to 100ms depending on the implementation) is recognized not as an interrupt request (IRQ_HPD), but as a physical disconnection event.
  • The GPU's display controller registers this transition via hardware interrupt immediately.

AUX Channel and Main-Link Teardown

DisplayPort separates audio/video delivery (Main Link) from control communication (Auxiliary Channel, or AUX CH). A sudden power loss halts both:

  1. AUX Channel Communication Failure: Any pending read/write transactions to the sink's DPCD or Extended Display Identification Data (EDID) over the AUX channel fail with an immediate timeout or NACK (negative acknowledge).
  2. Main-Link Transmission Halt: The source GPU deactivates the high-speed differential lanes used for video data. It stops link training patterns and clock transmission to avoid transmitting data into an unpowered, un-terminated load, which prevents electrical reflections and unnecessary GPU compute overhead.

OS and Desktop Topology Re-enumeration

Once the graphics driver confirms the loss of HPD and the failure of AUX handshaking, it signals the operating system's window management system:

  • Display Enumeration: The OS removes the disconnected display from the active monitor list.
  • Window Repositioning: Unlike legacy analog connections (like VGA) that remained persistent despite power states, DisplayPort strictly enforces state consistency. As a result, the OS automatically gathers all open application windows and desktop icons previously located on the disconnected screen and moves them to the primary or remaining active displays.
  • Rendering Pipeline Adjustments: The graphics pipeline scales down the desktop canvas and frees framebuffers associated with the disconnected display's native resolution and refresh rate.

Multi-Stream Transport (MST) Dynamics

In a Multi-Stream Transport (daisy-chain) environment, a sudden power loss depends on the monitor's position in the chain:

  • End-Point (Leaf) Monitor Loss: If the last monitor in the chain loses power, the upstream hub notifies the source using an IRQ_HPD message over the AUX channel. The GPU reallocates the available link bandwidth without disrupting upstream monitors.
  • Intermediate (Branch) Device Loss: If an intermediate monitor providing the daisy-chain hub loses power, all downstream displays drop simultaneously. The upstream connection reports a broken link, and the GPU tears down the virtual channels for all affected displays downstream.

Recovery Upon Power Restoration

When power returns to the monitor, the device initializes its internal scaler and asserts the HPD line back to a high state (+3.3V). The GPU detects this rising edge, reads the monitor's EDID and DPCD registers via the AUX channel, executes the full Link Training routine (adjusting voltage swing and pre-emphasis levels), and begins streaming video data again.
