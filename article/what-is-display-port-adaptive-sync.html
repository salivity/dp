What Is DisplayPort Adaptive-Sync?

DisplayPort Adaptive-Sync is a variable refresh rate (VRR) technology that dynamically aligns a display's refresh cycle to the rendering speed of a connected graphics processing unit (GPU). This article covers how the Adaptive-Sync protocol works, its technical benefits in eliminating visual artifacts, and the exact timeline of its inclusion in the official VESA DisplayPort specification.

What Is the DisplayPort Adaptive-Sync Protocol?

The DisplayPort Adaptive-Sync protocol is an open display standard established by the Video Electronics Standards Association (VESA). In traditional display configurations, a monitor refreshes its screen at a fixed frequency (such as 60Hz, 144Hz, or 240Hz), regardless of when the GPU finishes rendering a new frame. This mismatch causes two primary visual issues:

  • Screen Tearing: When a GPU delivers a new frame in the middle of a monitor's refresh cycle, resulting in split-frame visuals.
  • Stuttering and Input Lag: When traditional V-Sync forces the GPU to hold a rendered frame until the monitor is ready, introducing noticeable latency and frame drops.

Adaptive-Sync eliminates these issues by transferring refresh rate control from the display to the GPU. With Adaptive-Sync enabled, the monitor only refreshes its screen when the graphics card has completely rendered a frame. This creates a smooth, tear-free viewing experience with minimal input latency.

Adaptive-Sync also serves as the open foundational protocol for commercial implementations, including AMD FreeSync and NVIDIA’s "G-SYNC Compatible" program.

When Was It Added to the Core Standard?

VESA officially added Adaptive-Sync to the core DisplayPort standard with the release of DisplayPort version 1.2a in May 2014.

Before its inclusion in the external DisplayPort standard, the underlying technology existed as a power-saving feature in Embedded DisplayPort (eDP) for laptops, where lowering the display refresh rate reduced battery consumption when viewing static content. Recognizing the gaming and multimedia potential of variable refresh rates, VESA adapted the technology for standalone desktop monitors and external GPUs in the DP 1.2a update.

Adaptive-Sync has remained a permanent, core component in subsequent DisplayPort revisions, including DisplayPort 1.3, 1.4, 2.0, and 2.1.
