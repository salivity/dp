PCB Dielectrics for DisplayPort 2.1 Beyond FR-4

DisplayPort 2.1 introduces Ultra-High Bit Rate (UHBR) transmission modes—UHBR10, UHBR13.5, and UHBR20—capable of delivering up to 20 Gbps per lane and an aggregate bandwidth of 80 Gbps. At these elevated signaling rates, standard FR-4 dielectric materials exhibit excessive attenuation, dielectric absorption, and phase jitter, rendering them unsuitable for reliable signal delivery. This article details the advanced, low-loss dielectric substrates recommended for routing DisplayPort 2.1 traces, evaluates their physical and electrical properties, and outlines critical stackup design considerations.

The Limitations of Standard FR-4 at UHBR Speeds

DisplayPort 2.1 signals operating in UHBR20 mode run at a fundamental Nyquist frequency of 10 GHz. Standard FR-4 possesses a high dissipation factor (\(\tan\delta\) or \(D_f\)) typically between 0.015 and 0.020, along with a dielectric constant (\(D_k\)) around 4.2 to 4.5.

At 10 GHz, dielectric loss dominates over conductor skin effect loss. Utilizing FR-4 at these frequencies causes massive insertion loss across trace lengths greater than an inch or two, severe eye closure, and inter-symbol interference (ISI). To satisfy the strict compliance eye masks and reach requirements of DisplayPort 2.1, materials with significantly lower loss profiles must be used.

Recommended High-Performance Substrates

1. Very Low-Loss (VLL) Thermoset Resins

Very Low-Loss materials are the primary industry choice for high-speed digital interconnects like PCIe 5.0, DisplayPort 2.1, and 100GbE per lane implementations.

  • Panasonic Megtron 6: Widely regarded as the industry benchmark for ultra-high-speed routing up to 25 Gbps per channel. It features a \(D_f\) of approximately 0.002 to 0.004 and a \(D_k\) of 3.4 to 3.7 at 10 GHz. Megtron 6 provides thermal stability, high glass transition temperature (\(T_g\)), and low moisture absorption.
  • Isola Tachyon 100G / I-Tera MT40: Designed specifically for high-speed digital designs requiring high thermal endurance and minimal dielectric loss. Tachyon 100G maintains a stable \(D_k\) (around 3.02) and a \(D_f\) of approximately 0.0021 up to 40 GHz, making it an excellent candidate for long-trace DP 2.1 designs.
  • Nelco N4000-13 / N4000-13 EPSI: A reinforced epoxy resin system offering a \(D_f\) around 0.008 to 0.009 (standard) down to 0.006 (EPSI). While higher in loss than Megtron 6, it can serve as a cost-effective option for shorter trace lengths (UHBR10 or localized UHBR13.5 routing).

2. Ultra-Low-Loss (ULL) Resins

For extended cable driver circuits, docking stations, or long routing runs where insertion loss budgets are extremely tight, Ultra-Low-Loss laminates are required.

  • Panasonic Megtron 7 and Megtron 8: These formulations push loss factors down further (\(D_f \approx 0.0015\) at 10 GHz), offering superior low transmission loss, near-zero dispersion, and minimal signal skew over temperature fluctuations.
  • Rogers RO4000 Series (e.g., RO4350B, RO4835): Hydrocarbon ceramic laminates traditionally used in RF designs. They offer exceptional high-frequency performance (\(D_f \approx 0.0037\), \(D_k \approx 3.48\)) and rigid mechanical properties. They are often applied selectively in hybrid constructions due to processing complexities and cost.

Critical Substrate Properties Beyond Base Resins

Selecting the correct resin system is only part of maintaining signal integrity at 20 Gbps per lane. The physical makeup of the substrate must also address high-frequency parasitics:

  • Spread Glass Weave: Standard E-glass fabric creates alternating localized windows of glass fiber and resin. Because the \(D_k\) of glass (\(\approx 6.0\)) differs significantly from the resin (\(\approx 3.0\)), differential pairs routed across non-uniform weave suffer from fiber-weave-induced skew. Substrates specified for DisplayPort 2.1 must use mechanically flattened, spread-glass fabrics (e.g., glass styles 1067, 1086, or 3313) to ensure a uniform \(D_k\) distribution.
  • Copper Profile (HVLP/VLP): At 10 GHz, the skin depth of copper is less than 0.7 microns. Surface roughness on the foil creates significant conductor loss. Specifying Very Low Profile (VLP) or Hyper Very Low Profile (HVLP) copper foils with roughness (\(R_z\)) below 1.5 µm is mandatory to prevent conductor losses from negating the advantages of the low-loss dielectric.

Cost-Effective Implementation: Hybrid Stackups

Full-board fabrication using high-grade materials like Megtron 6 or Tachyon 100G across high-layer-count motherboards or graphics cards can be cost-prohibitive.

Hardware designers typically implement hybrid stackups. In a hybrid configuration, ultra-low-loss laminates are assigned exclusively to the outer microstrip layers or critical inner stripline layers where DisplayPort 2.1 differential pairs travel. The inner power, ground, and low-speed digital layers utilize mid-loss or standard FR-4 cores. This approach controls manufacturing costs while confining the UHBR signals to an optimal, low-attenuation environment.
