DisplayPort Insertion Loss Limits on FR-4 Boards

Designing printed circuit boards for high-speed DisplayPort interfaces requires strict adherence to insertion loss budgets to maintain signal integrity and ensure VESA compliance. On standard FR-4 material, maximum permissible insertion loss is governed by the targeted DisplayPort generation, the corresponding Nyquist frequency, and the allocation designated specifically for the host or sink PCB trace segment. While total channel budgets—including cables and connectors—reach up to -24 dB in modern standards, the host PCB trace on FR-4 is typically constrained between -3.0 dB and -6.5 dB depending on the operational bit rate.

VESA Loss Allocation for PCB Traces

The DisplayPort physical layer standard divides the total end-to-end channel insertion loss across the source PCB, the connector, the physical cable assembly, and the sink PCB. System designers targeting standard FR-4 must design within the budget assigned to the source or sink board alone:

  • HBR (2.7 Gbps / 1.35 GHz Nyquist): Permissible source PCB loss is typically capped at -3.0 dB to -3.5 dB.
  • HBR2 (5.4 Gbps / 2.7 GHz Nyquist): Permissible source PCB loss is limited to -4.0 dB to -4.5 dB.
  • HBR3 (8.1 Gbps / 4.05 GHz Nyquist): The normative loss budget allows up to -6.0 dB to -6.5 dB on the host board, provided the remaining channel components stay strictly within their allocated margins. Most production designs target less than -4.5 dB at 4.05 GHz to guarantee signal margin.
  • UHBR 10 / 13.5 / 20 (DP 2.1): Total host-side channel routing (bump-to-receptacle) is typically allocated -5.0 dB to -7.0 dB at the respective Nyquist frequency (e.g., 5 GHz for UHBR 10), leveraging continuous-time linear equalization (CTLE) and decision feedback equalization (DFE).

Standard FR-4 Physical Limitations

Standard FR-4 possesses a dielectric constant (\(D_k\)) of approximately 4.2 to 4.5 and a dissipation factor (\(D_f\)) between 0.015 and 0.020. At high frequencies, dielectric loss and skin effect attenuation scale rapidly.

  • At 2.7 GHz (HBR2), standard FR-4 exhibits an insertion loss of roughly 0.6 dB to 0.8 dB per inch for a standard 100-ohm differential pair. Trace lengths should not exceed 5 to 6 inches.
  • At 4.05 GHz (HBR3), attenuation increases to approximately 0.9 dB to 1.3 dB per inch. Trace lengths on standard FR-4 must be kept below 4 to 5 inches to prevent exceeding the -6.0 dB threshold.
  • For UHBR rates, routing more than 2 to 3 inches on standard FR-4 often exceeds the loss and phase consistency limits, generally requiring an upgrade to mid-loss or low-loss laminates (such as Nelco or Megtron) or the addition of redrivers/retimers.

Practical Layout Considerations

To remain within the permissible insertion loss envelope on standard FR-4:

  1. Maintain Controlled Impedance: Ensure differential traces strictly adhere to 100 ohms (or 85 ohms where specified in DP 2.1) with minimal tolerance variations.
  2. Minimize Vias and Stubs: Every via transition introduces parasitic capacitance and inductance, typically adding 0.2 dB to 0.5 dB of loss plus severe resonance dips if stubs are left un-backdrilled.
  3. Use Wider Traces Where Feasible: Slightly widening the trace geometry while maintaining the target differential impedance reduces conductor losses (skin effect).
  4. Account for Copper Roughness: Standard electrodeposited (ED) copper causes elevated losses above 3 GHz. Specify reverse-treated foil (RTF) or very low profile (VLP) copper when utilizing FR-4 at HBR3 speeds.
