How Much Latency Does DisplayPort DSC Add?

Display Stream Compression (DSC) introduces virtually imperceptible latency into a DisplayPort video pipeline, adding only a fraction of a millisecond to the total signal path. Developed by VESA as a visually lossless, low-complexity standard, DSC utilizes real-time, line-based processing rather than full-frame buffering. As a result, the combined hardware compression and decompression cycle introduces a delay measured strictly in microseconds, making it practically non-existent for everyday computing, competitive gaming, and professional applications.

The Latency Numbers

In a hardware-implemented DisplayPort pipeline, the total end-to-end latency added by the DSC compression (encoder on the GPU) and decompression (decoder in the display scaler) cycle is typically under 10 to 50 microseconds (0.01 to 0.05 milliseconds).

To put this into perspective:

  • At 60 Hz, one frame takes 16.67 ms.
  • At 144 Hz, one frame takes 6.94 ms.
  • At 240 Hz, one frame takes 4.17 ms.

A delay of 0.05 ms represents less than 1% of a single frame at 144 Hz, which is well below the threshold of human perception and significantly lower than typical display panel response times or pixel transition times.

Why DSC Latency Is So Low

Traditional video compression algorithms (such as H.264, H.265, or AV1) achieve high compression ratios by buffering multiple entire frames to analyze temporal changes over time. This frame buffering introduces tens of milliseconds of latency.

In contrast, VESA DSC is designed specifically for display interfaces and operates strictly as a intra-frame, line-based codec:

  • Scan-Line Buffering: Hardware DSC encoders and decoders buffer only a few horizontal scan lines of pixels at a time (often fewer than three to four lines) to determine predictive color differences and entropy.
  • Stream-Through Architecture: Compression occurs on-the-fly as the GPU’s display engine rasterizes the image, and decompression occurs simultaneously inside the monitor's timing controller (TCON) as the pixel data arrives.

Because the display does not need to wait for an entire frame to arrive before decoding begins, the cycle achieves true real-time throughput.

Impact on Gaming and Real-Time Systems

Because hardware DSC operates at sub-millisecond speeds, it does not introduce any detectable input lag. Competitive esports players and professional colorists can run high-resolution, high-refresh-rate configurations—such as 4K at 144 Hz or 240 Hz over DisplayPort 1.4 or 2.1—with DSC enabled without compromising latency or visual integrity compared to an uncompressed native stream.
