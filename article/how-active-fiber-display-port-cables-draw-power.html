How Active Fiber DisplayPort Cables Draw Power

Active optical DisplayPort cables (AOCs) replace traditional copper wires with optical fibers to transmit high-bandwidth audio and video signals over extended distances without degradation. Because fiber optics rely on light rather than electrical pulses, these cables house integrated microchips, laser transmitters, and photodetectors directly inside their connector housings. To operate these internal electro-optical components without needing external power adapters, the cables draw low-voltage electrical current directly from the connected video source via standardized DisplayPort connector pins and hybrid internal wiring.

The Role of DisplayPort Pin 20 (DP_PWR)

Standard DisplayPort interfaces feature 20 pins, with Pin 20 specifically designated as DP_PWR. In standard passive copper-to-copper cables, Pin 20 is intentionally left disconnected to prevent hardware damage from voltage mismatches between two powered devices (such as a graphics card and a monitor).

However, active optical cables deliberately utilize Pin 20. The source device (such as a PC graphics card, laptop dock, or media server) is required by the VESA DisplayPort specification to supply a regulated +3.3V DC on Pin 20, providing at least 500 mA (approximately 1.5 watts to 1.65 watts). Active fiber cables route this power directly into the internal circuitry of the source-side connector head.

Powering the Transmitters: The TX Module

Inside the source-end DisplayPort connector is a Transmitter (TX) module consisting of:

  • A driver integrated circuit (IC) that cleans and equalizes incoming electrical video signals.
  • An array of Vertical-Cavity Surface-Emitting Lasers (VCSELs).

When connected, the TX module immediately draws the 3.3V current from Pin 20. This power drives the laser diodes, which convert the electrical data packets from the GPU into modulated light pulses for high-speed transmission down the glass fibers.

Hybrid Cable Construction: Sending Power to the RX Module

Light cannot carry electrical current to power the photodiode receivers at the display (sink) end of the cable. To solve this, active optical DisplayPort cables utilize a hybrid construction.

Along with the core optical fibers that transmit the primary high-speed data lanes, the cable jacket contains dedicated, ultra-thin insulated copper conductors. These copper wires carry:

  1. Direct Current (DC) Power: Electrical current from the source’s Pin 20 travels across the length of the cable alongside the fibers.
  2. Auxiliary (AUX) and Hot Plug Detect (HPD) Channels: Low-frequency handshaking and management signals that do not require optical conversion.

Once the current reaches the display connector, it powers the Receiver (RX) module—including the photodiode array and transimpedance amplifiers—which decodes the optical pulses back into standard electrical DisplayPort signals readable by the monitor.

Power Management and Device Compliance

Because active fiber cables draw operating power entirely from the host, their functionality depends on the source device providing a compliant, stable voltage on Pin 20. Modern graphics cards and professional AV equipment adhere strictly to VESA power output standards, allowing active optical cables to run purely as plug-and-play devices that operate invisibly to the user without external power bricks or battery packs.
