DisplayPort DP80 Certification Explained

The DP80 certification badge on modern DisplayPort cables indicates that the cable is officially certified by the Video Electronics Standards Association (VESA) to support a maximum bandwidth of 80 gigabits per second (Gbps). Introduced to support DisplayPort 2.0 and DisplayPort 2.1 specifications, this badge provides a clear, standardized performance guarantee that the cable can handle maximum data throughput without signal degradation, supporting extreme resolutions, higher refresh rates, and advanced color depth for high-end displays and graphics hardware.

What the DP80 Badge Represents

The DP80 label signifies that a cable supports the highest transmission tier established by VESA: Ultra-High Bit Rate 20 (UHBR20). A standard DisplayPort connection utilizes four physical data lanes. In a DP80 cable, each lane can transmit 20 Gbps of data, resulting in a combined maximum raw bandwidth of 80 Gbps.

This throughput represents nearly triple the bandwidth of the previous DisplayPort 1.4a standard, which maxed out at 32.4 Gbps (HBR3). In practical terms, this bandwidth enables:

  • Single-display configurations up to 16K resolution at 60Hz using Display Stream Compression (DSC).
  • Uncompressed 8K resolution at 60Hz with full 10-bit color and HDR.
  • 4K resolution at refresh rates exceeding 240Hz for competitive gaming.
  • Simultaneous multi-monitor configurations at high resolutions through a single cable or hub.

The Purpose of VESA's Performance Badging

VESA introduced the DP80 badge—alongside the lower-tier DP40 badge (which supports 40 Gbps via UHBR10)—to eliminate consumer confusion. Previously, cables were often marketed using specification version numbers like "DisplayPort 1.4" or "DisplayPort 2.0." Because DisplayPort specifications define both mandatory and optional features, knowing the version number did not guarantee that a cable supported the maximum available bandwidth.

By replacing ambiguous version numbers with direct bandwidth ratings, the DP80 badge serves as a direct indicator of capability. If a cable bears the official DP80 logo, it has undergone rigorous testing at a VESA-authorized test center to ensure signal integrity at full 80 Gbps speeds.

Physical Connectors and Compatibility

DP80 certification is not limited to a single physical design. The badge applies across multiple implementations:

  • Standard DisplayPort: The traditional full-sized DisplayPort connector.
  • Mini DisplayPort: Smaller implementations often used on laptops and graphics cards.
  • USB Type-C: Cables supporting DisplayPort Alternate Mode (DP Alt Mode) that can operate at UHBR20 speeds across the USB-C interface.

DP80 cables remain fully backward compatible with older DisplayPort devices. When connected to legacy monitors or source hardware, a DP80 cable will simply step down its transmission speed to match the maximum capability of the connected equipment.
