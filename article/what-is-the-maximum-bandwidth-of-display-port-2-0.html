What Is the Maximum Bandwidth of DisplayPort 2.0?

The DisplayPort 2.0 standard dramatically expands display connection capabilities, delivering a theoretical maximum link bandwidth of 80 Gbps across four lanes. With the introduction of the more efficient 128b/132b channel encoding, it achieves a maximum usable payload data rate of 77.37 Gbps. This leap enables support for resolutions up to 16K, multi-monitor 4K configurations at high refresh rates, and uncompressed high dynamic range (HDR) workflows.

Link Bandwidth vs. Usable Data Rate

The DisplayPort 2.0 specification, released by the Video Electronics Standards Association (VESA), structures transmission into raw link bandwidth and effective data rate.

  • Theoretical Maximum Link Bandwidth: 80.00 Gbps (20.00 Gbps per lane across 4 lanes).
  • Maximum Effective Data Payload: 77.37 Gbps.

Earlier DisplayPort revisions (such as DP 1.4a) used 8b/10b channel coding, which incurred a 20% transmission overhead. In contrast, DisplayPort 2.0 transitioned to 128b/132b channel encoding. This scheme reduces encoding overhead to roughly 3%, allowing approximately 97% of the raw 80 Gbps link bandwidth to carry actual image, audio, and sync data.

Ultra High Bit Rate (UHBR) Transmission Tiers

DisplayPort 2.0 introduced three distinct Ultra High Bit Rate (UHBR) transmission standards to accommodate varying hardware tiers and cable types:

  • UHBR 10: 10 Gbps per lane, resulting in a 40 Gbps raw link bandwidth and a 38.69 Gbps maximum data rate (marketed as DP40).
  • UHBR 13.5: 13.5 Gbps per lane, offering a 54 Gbps raw link bandwidth and a 52.22 Gbps maximum data rate.
  • UHBR 20: 20 Gbps per lane, hitting the full 80 Gbps raw link bandwidth and 77.37 Gbps maximum data rate (marketed as DP80).

Real-World Display Configurations

The bandwidth headroom provided by UHBR 20 enables demanding display configurations over a single connection:

  • Single Display: Uncompressed 10K (10240×4320) at 60 Hz with 24-bit color, or up to 16K (15360×8640) at 60 Hz with 30-bit HDR using Display Stream Compression (DSC 1.2a).
  • Dual Displays: Two uncompressed 4K displays at 144 Hz, or two 8K displays at 120 Hz using DSC.
  • Triple Displays: Three uncompressed 4K displays at 90 Hz, or three 10K displays at 60 Hz with DSC.

Beyond traditional DisplayPort connectors, the 80 Gbps physical layer is also mapped over USB-C via DisplayPort Alternate Mode and forms the foundational display architecture for modern high-speed interface protocols.
