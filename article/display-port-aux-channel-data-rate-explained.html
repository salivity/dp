DisplayPort AUX Channel Data Rate Explained

The DisplayPort Auxiliary (AUX) channel is a dedicated, bidirectional communication pathway used for link management, device discovery, and status monitoring between a source and a display. The standard data rate for this channel is 1 Mbps, which handles essential metadata such as Extended Display Identification Data (EDID) and DisplayPort Configuration Data (DPCD). This article explains the technical specifications of the standard AUX channel, its underlying encoding, its role in the DisplayPort standard, and the higher-speed alternatives available.

Standard Data Rate and Signaling

The standard DisplayPort bidirectional AUX channel operates at a data rate of 1 Mbps (megabit per second). It functions as a half-duplex link over a single AC-coupled differential pair of wires.

To ensure robust signal integrity and synchronization without requiring a separate clock line, the channel uses Manchester II encoding:

  • Raw Bit Rate: 2 Mbaud (mega-transfers per second).
  • Effective Bit Rate: 1 Mbps, due to the 50% efficiency inherent to Manchester encoding (each data bit requires two transitions).
  • Signaling: AC-coupled, low-voltage differential signaling with a typical amplitude of several hundred millivolts.

Because the link is half-duplex, devices take turns transmitting and receiving, using a strict transaction-based request-and-response protocol.

Primary Functions of the AUX Channel

The 1 Mbps bandwidth is intentionally kept modest to prioritize reliability and low power consumption over high throughput. The main video and audio streams travel across high-speed main links, while the AUX channel is reserved for:

  • Link Training: Configuring the main link lanes, data rates (e.g., RBR, HBR, UHBR), and drive levels.
  • EDID / DisplayID Retrieval: Reading monitor capabilities, supported resolutions, refresh rates, and color profiles.
  • DPCD Communication: Reading receiver capabilities, error counts, and link status.
  • HDCP Handshaking: Exchanging encryption keys and authenticating content protection.
  • MCCS (Monitor Control Command Set): Allowing the host to adjust monitor settings such as brightness and contrast via software.

Fast AUX (FAUX) and Newer Standards

With the release of DisplayPort 1.2, VESA introduced an optional extension known as Fast AUX (FAUX).

  • Speed: Up to 720 Mbps.
  • Purpose: Designed to carry supplementary data types, such as USB 2.0 traffic or Ethernet over the DisplayPort connector.

Despite the addition of Fast AUX, the standard 1 Mbps Manchester-encoded AUX channel remains mandatory across all modern versions of DisplayPort, including DisplayPort 1.4 and DisplayPort 2.1. This guarantees universal backward compatibility and deterministic link setup across all cables, adapters, and host devices.
