DisplayPort Adaptive-Sync Minimum Refresh Rate

DisplayPort Adaptive-Sync establishes a dynamic refresh rate window where the monitor synchronizes with the GPU's frame output. The minimum refresh rate threshold supported by most Adaptive-Sync displays before Low Framerate Compensation (LFC) activates is typically 48 Hz, with some panels bottoming out at 40 Hz or occasionally 30 Hz. When the frame rate drops below this hardware-defined lower limit, native 1:1 frame synchronization ends, and LFC engages to maintain tear-free, smooth gameplay.

The Standard Adaptive-Sync Operating Window

Every variable refresh rate (VRR) monitor has a physical operating range. Common panels feature ranges such as 48 Hz to 144 Hz, 48 Hz to 165 Hz, or 40 Hz to 240 Hz. The lower boundary represents the slowest rate at which the panel's internal scaler and liquid crystals can refresh without causing visible artifacts, such as brightness flickering.

Because standard display hardware struggles to refresh smoothly at extremely low frequencies, 48 Hz serves as the prevailing baseline across mainstream monitors. On higher-tier displays featuring advanced scalar hardware, this threshold can lower to 40 Hz or 30 Hz, but 48 Hz remains the industry standard lower boundary for DisplayPort-certified displays.

How LFC Activates Below the Threshold

When your GPU delivers frame rates that match or exceed the minimum threshold, Adaptive-Sync refreshes the panel once per frame. Once the frame rate drops below that baseline (e.g., dipping to 35 FPS on a 48–144 Hz monitor), native synchronization becomes impossible.

At this point, Low Framerate Compensation automatically activates:

  1. Frame Duplication: The graphics driver instructs the monitor to display each generated frame multiple times.
  2. Frequency Multiplication: If a game runs at 30 FPS on a 48 Hz minimum display, LFC doubles the frames to display at 60 Hz (30 FPS × 2). If the game drops to 20 FPS, it may triple the output to 60 Hz (20 FPS × 3).
  3. VRR Maintenance: By keeping the effective refresh rate within the supported operating window (e.g., 60 Hz falls inside 48–144 Hz), the display prevents the severe stuttering and screen tearing associated with traditional V-Sync.

Requirements for LFC Support

LFC is not universal across all Adaptive-Sync monitors. For LFC to function properly, the display's maximum refresh rate must typically be at least double (2x), and ideally 2.4 times, the minimum refresh rate.

  • LFC Capable: A 48 Hz to 144 Hz display has a 3:1 ratio, easily satisfying the requirement for smooth frame multiplication.
  • Non-LFC Capable: A narrow-range display running from 48 Hz to 75 Hz lacks the bandwidth to double lower frame rates without exceeding its 75 Hz ceiling, meaning LFC cannot engage and the display must fall back to standard V-Sync behavior or introduce tearing below 48 Hz.
