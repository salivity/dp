Max 1080p Monitors Daisy Chained with DisplayPort 1.2

This article explains the maximum number of 1080p monitors you can daisy-chain using a single DisplayPort 1.2 connection. It breaks down the bandwidth specifications of the DisplayPort standard, how Multi-Stream Transport (MST) functions, and the practical hardware limitations imposed by graphics cards and operating systems that impact your multi-monitor setup.

The Maximum Limit: 4 Monitors

Under the DisplayPort 1.2 specification, the maximum number of daisy-chained 1080p (1920x1080) monitors running at a standard 60Hz refresh rate is four.

This limitation is primarily determined by the available data bandwidth of the DisplayPort 1.2 standard and the data transmission requirements of a standard Full HD display.

Bandwidth Breakdown

DisplayPort 1.2 introduced Multi-Stream Transport (MST), a technology that allows multiple independent video signals to be transmitted across a single cable.

  • Total Usable Bandwidth: DisplayPort 1.2 offers a maximum total bandwidth of 21.6 Gbps, with an effective maximum data throughput of 17.28 Gbps after encoding overhead.
  • 1080p @ 60Hz Requirement: A standard 1080p display at 60Hz with 8-bit color depth requires approximately 3.20 to 4.46 Gbps of bandwidth, depending on the blanking intervals used by the monitor.
  • The Math: Dividing the 17.28 Gbps usable bandwidth by the bandwidth required per monitor allocates enough capacity for four simultaneous displays (roughly 22% of total bandwidth per monitor).

Hardware Requirements and Limitations

While the DisplayPort 1.2 standard supports four 1080p displays, several hardware requirements must be met to achieve this configuration:

  1. Monitor Compatibility: Every monitor in the chain except the last one must feature both a DisplayPort In port and a DisplayPort Out port. The final monitor in the chain only requires a DisplayPort In port.
  2. GPU Display Pipelines: Your graphics processing unit (GPU) must support at least four active displays. Many integrated GPUs (such as older Intel graphics) only support up to three total displays, regardless of available DisplayPort bandwidth. Dedicated GPUs from NVIDIA and AMD typically support four or more displays.
  3. Operating System Support: MST daisy-chaining is fully supported on Windows and ChromeOS. macOS does not natively support DisplayPort MST daisy-chaining; connecting multiple monitors via a daisy chain on a Mac will result in mirrored displays rather than extended desktops.

Impact of Higher Refresh Rates

The four-monitor limit strictly applies to standard 60Hz displays. Increasing the refresh rate significantly increases bandwidth demands:

  • 1080p at 120Hz: Consumes twice the bandwidth of 60Hz, reducing the chain to a maximum of two monitors.
  • 1080p at 144Hz: Requires higher throughput that limits a DisplayPort 1.2 daisy chain to one or two monitors, depending on timing and blanking parameters.
