USB4 Bandwidth Allocation: PCIe vs DisplayPort

USB4 dynamically manages shared high-speed links by encapsulating diverse protocols into a single transport layer. This article explores how a USB4 host allocates shared bandwidth between tunneled DisplayPort (DP) and PCI Express (PCIe) traffic, detailing the role of the USB4 Connection Manager, dynamic bandwidth arbitration, and protocol prioritization.

The Role of Protocol Tunneling

USB4 does not transmit native DisplayPort or PCIe signals directly across the physical wires. Instead, it uses protocol tunneling. The USB4 host controller contains integrated adapters that convert native DisplayPort main link streams and PCIe packets into USB4 Transport Layer packets. Because both protocols share the same physical link (typically up to 20 Gbps, 40 Gbps, or 80 Gbps depending on the generation), the host must arbitrate data transmission to prevent starvation and ensure signal integrity.

Bandwidth Arbitration by the Connection Manager

The USB4 Connection Manager—a software or firmware layer running on the host system—is responsible for discovering devices, establishing paths, and allocating bandwidth across the USB4 fabric.

Bandwidth allocation in USB4 is dynamic rather than statically divided. Instead of permanently assigning half of the physical lanes to PCIe and half to DisplayPort, the Connection Manager configures the internal switches to multiplex packets on a shared pipe based on active demands and traffic priority.

DisplayPort Traffic: Reserved and Isochronous

DisplayPort carries isochronous traffic, meaning video and audio data require guaranteed delivery times and continuous throughput to prevent frame dropping, visual artifacts, or black screens.

  1. Bandwidth Reservation: When a monitor is connected, the DisplayPort adapter negotiates the required video bandwidth (determined by resolution, refresh rate, and color depth) via the tunneled AUX channel.
  2. Priority Guarantee: The Connection Manager sets aside the precise amount of bandwidth required for the active display mode. Once established, this bandwidth is effectively guaranteed to the DisplayPort path, preventing other protocols from interrupting the display pipeline.
  3. DisplayPort Bandwidth Allocation (DP BWA): USB4 supports DP BWA, allowing the host to dynamically renegotiate and reclaim unused DisplayPort bandwidth if display requirements change—such as when a monitor enters sleep mode or switches to a lower refresh rate.

PCIe Traffic: Asynchronous and Opportunistic

Unlike DisplayPort, standard PCIe data traffic is asynchronous and inherently bursty. Storage transfers, external GPUs, and network cards operate on a packet-switched model that tolerates variable latency and fluctuating throughput.

  1. Best-Effort Utilization: PCIe traffic utilizes the "best-effort" capacity left over after isochronous DisplayPort streams have claimed their guaranteed share.
  2. Elastic Scaling: If no displays are active, PCIe can consume nearly the entire usable link capacity (minus protocol overhead and any active USB3 tunneling). If a high-resolution display is attached, the bandwidth available to PCIe dynamically contracts, throttling PCIe throughput down to the remaining headroom without interrupting the connection.

Transport Layer Scheduling and QoS

At the hardware level, the USB4 Host Router relies on internal Quality of Service (QoS) mechanisms and credit-based flow control to enforce bandwidth rules:

  • Egress Queuing: Outgoing physical adapters maintain distinct queues for different traffic classes.
  • Packet Scheduling: Tunneled DisplayPort packets are assigned a higher scheduling priority than PCIe packets. When a DisplayPort packet arrives at the router, the scheduler dispatches it ahead of waiting PCIe bulk transfer packets to satisfy strict timing deadlines.
  • Flow Control Credits: PCIe adapters use buffer-to-buffer credit mechanisms. If DisplayPort traffic saturates the link, the host reduces available credits to the PCIe adapter, naturally throttling host-side PCIe packet generation until link bandwidth clears.
