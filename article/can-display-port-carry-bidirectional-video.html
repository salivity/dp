Can DisplayPort Carry Bidirectional Video?

A standard DisplayPort cable cannot carry bidirectional video simultaneously over the same physical connection. DisplayPort’s hardware architecture uses unidirectional high-speed lanes to transmit high-bandwidth audio and video signals strictly from a source device to a display device. While DisplayPort does incorporate a bidirectional communication channel, its bandwidth is restricted to control data and handshake protocols rather than active video feeds.

The Main Link: A One-Way Highway

The primary reason DisplayPort cannot transmit video in both directions simultaneously lies in the design of its Main Link. The Main Link consists of one, two, or four AC-coupled differential pairs (lanes). In native DisplayPort connectors:

  • All high-speed lanes are physically directed to route data exclusively from the transmitter (source, such as a graphics card) to the receiver (sink, such as a monitor).
  • The physical layer and signaling standards (from DP 1.0 through DP 2.1) do not support full-duplex transmission or lane reversal on the native DisplayPort connector.
  • Because the bandwidth across these lanes is entirely allocated to the outbound display signal, no bandwidth remains for incoming video.

The Role of the AUX Channel

DisplayPort does feature a bidirectional channel called the Auxiliary (AUX) channel. However, it cannot be used to transmit simultaneous reverse video:

  • Bandwidth Limitations: The standard AUX channel operates at roughly 1 Mbps (or up to 720 Mbps in newer revisions using USB-C tunneling). This is far below the multi-gigabit bandwidth required for raw or lightly compressed video streams.
  • Intended Purpose: The AUX channel is a half-duplex link designed strictly for sideband management, including Extended Display Identification Data (EDID), DisplayPort Configuration Data (DPCD), and High-bandwidth Digital Content Protection (HDCP) handshakes.

Multi-Stream Transport (MST) Is Not Bidirectional

DisplayPort 1.2 introduced Multi-Stream Transport (MST), which enables daisy-chaining multiple monitors through a single cable connection. While this allows multiple video streams over one physical cable, it is entirely unidirectional. The video signal originates at the host PC, passes to the primary display, and is forwarded downstream to subsequent displays. At no point can a connected display send a video signal back up the chain to the host.

Alternatives Supporting Bidirectional Protocols

Users requiring simultaneous bidirectional data exchange typically use technologies that encapsulate DisplayPort within a broader transport protocol:

  • Thunderbolt (3, 4, and 5): Uses active controllers to route bidirectional, high-speed PCIe and DisplayPort data over a single USB-C cable. While individual DisplayPort tunnels inside Thunderbolt remain inherently directional, the overall physical cable can simultaneously carry video outbound while receiving high-bandwidth data inbound.
  • USB4: Implements protocol tunneling similar to Thunderbolt, allowing dynamic allocation of high-speed lanes for combined transmit and receive workloads.
