Top DisplayPort to HDMI Converter Chipsets

High-performance DisplayPort to HDMI converters rely on specialized Protocol Converter (PCON) chipsets to bridge the architectural divide between DisplayPort's packetized micro-packet architecture and HDMI’s Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) or Fixed Rate Link (FRL) signaling. This article breaks down the industry-standard chipsets developed by market leaders like Synaptics, Realtek, Parade Technologies, and Kinetic Technologies, detailing how these silicon components handle bandwidth-intensive tasks such as 4K at 120Hz, 8K at 60Hz, Display Stream Compression (DSC), and High-Bandwidth Digital Content Protection (HDCP) translation.

Why Dedicated Protocol Converter (PCON) Chipsets Are Required

Passive DisplayPort-to-HDMI adapters depend on the source device operating in "Dual-Mode" (DP++), which limits output to older HDMI standards (typically HDMI 1.4 or rudimentary HDMI 2.0 via TMDS). In contrast, high-performance active converters employ dedicated hardware PCONs. These chips ingest native DisplayPort Main Link data, de-packetize it, execute internal protocol translation, decrypt and re-encrypt HDCP streams, unpack DSC if necessary, and re-transmit the data across HDMI channels using high-speed signaling schemes like FRL (up to 48 Gbps).

Leading Chipset Manufacturers and Models

1. Synaptics (Cayman / VMM Series)

Synaptics is a primary supplier of protocol converters utilized in enthusiast-tier adapters and multi-display docks:

  • Synaptics VMM7100: Engineered specifically for the transition from DisplayPort 1.4a to HDMI 2.1. It supports 8K at 60Hz and 4K at 120Hz by processing DSC 1.2a streams and driving HDMI 2.1 FRL up to 12 Gbps across 4 lanes (48 Gbps total). It includes integrated support for Dynamic HDR, Dolby Vision pass-through, and HDCP 2.3 conversion.
  • Synaptics VMM6100: A streamlined counterpart to the 7100 series, commonly integrated into multi-stream transport (MST) hubs, facilitating high-resolution DisplayPort 1.4 conversions to HDMI 2.1 with full support for Variable Refresh Rate (VRR) and Auto Low Latency Mode (ALLM).

2. Realtek

Realtek offers highly competitive, cost-effective, and low-power bridge chips widely used in USB-C/DisplayPort dongles and cables:

  • Realtek RTD2173: A premier DP 1.4 to HDMI 2.1 converter IC. It supports resolutions up to 7680x4320 at 60Hz (with DSC) and 3840x2160 at 144Hz. The chip handles DP 1.4 HBR3 (High Bit Rate 3) inputs and outputs HDMI FRL data formats (3, 6, 8, 10, or 12 Gbps per lane), offering low power consumption suitable for bus-powered portable adapters.
  • Realtek RTD2172: The predecessor targeting the DisplayPort 1.4 to HDMI 2.0 standard. It translates HBR2/HBR3 data to HDMI TMDS up to 6 Gbps per lane (18 Gbps total), delivering uncompressed 4K at 60Hz with HDR10 support.

3. Parade Technologies

Parade Technologies is known for signal integrity solutions, retimers, and protocol converters used by major PC OEMs:

  • Parade PS196: A next-generation DisplayPort 2.1 to HDMI 2.1 protocol converter. It takes UHBR (Ultra-High Bit Rate) inputs and translates them into HDMI 2.1 FRL outputs, targeting workstations and ultra-high-bandwidth setups.
  • Parade PS188: A DP 1.4a to HDMI 2.1 converter with integrated jitter-cleaning circuitry. It includes an embedded MCU for firmware updates and handles complete DSC 1.2 decoding, enabling seamless 8K and 4K120 gaming conversions.
  • Parade PS186: The standard workhorse for DisplayPort 1.4 to HDMI 2.0b conversions. It features integrated level-shifting, TMDS conversion, and dual-mode jitter-reduction algorithms.

4. Kinetic Technologies (formerly MegaChips)

Kinetic supplies enterprise-grade video converter silicon commonly found in docking stations and server remote-management modules:

  • Kinetic MCDP2900: A dedicated DP 1.4 to HDMI 2.0b converter. It converts multi-lane DisplayPort Main Link signals up to HBR2 (5.4 Gbps per lane) into HDMI 2.0b TMDS outputs. It integrates an on-chip microprocessor running secure firmware for cryptographic key storage, managing HDCP 1.4 to HDCP 2.2 cross-conversion.

Core Hardware Capabilities of High-Performance PCONs

  • Display Stream Compression (DSC): Modern converters feature native DSC 1.2/1.2a engines, enabling the conversion of visually lossless compressed DisplayPort streams into uncompressed or differently packaged HDMI signals to preserve bandwidth.
  • HDCP Cross-Conversion: These chipsets bridge the gap between DisplayPort's content protection implementations and HDMI’s authentication schemes, seamlessly translating HDCP 2.2/2.3 sources to compliant displays.
  • Audio Protocol Bridging: Chips manage linear PCM, Dolby Atmos, and DTS:X bitstreams, mapping DisplayPort secondary-data audio packets directly onto HDMI audio data islands without introductorial latency or clock-drift issues.
