Can DisplayPort Output Multi-Channel LPCM to an AVR?

DisplayPort is fully capable of carrying raw, uncompressed multi-channel Linear Pulse Code Modulation (LPCM) audio to an AV receiver (AVR). The DisplayPort standard natively supports up to eight channels of uncompressed audio (5.1 or 7.1 surround sound) at high sample rates and bit depths. However, because the vast majority of consumer AV receivers only feature HDMI inputs rather than DisplayPort connections, successfully routing this audio requires the correct adapter hardware, graphics card capabilities, and operating system configuration.

Technical Capabilities of DisplayPort

DisplayPort specifications (from version 1.2 up to the latest standards like DisplayPort 2.1) have ample bandwidth for high-resolution audio alongside video. The protocol supports up to 8 channels of uncompressed LPCM audio at 24-bit depth and 192 kHz sampling rates. Because LPCM is an uncompressed digital audio format, it avoids the compression artifacts associated with lossy formats like standard Dolby Digital or DTS, delivering studio-grade multi-channel sound directly from the source to the receiver.

The DisplayPort to HDMI Connection

The primary obstacle in sending multi-channel LPCM from a computer to an AV receiver is the physical interface. AV receivers are built around HDMI, while computers and graphics cards commonly use DisplayPort.

To connect a DisplayPort output to an HDMI input on an AVR:

  • Passive Adapters (DP++): If the graphics card supports DisplayPort Dual-Mode (indicated by a DP++ icon), a passive DisplayPort-to-HDMI cable or adapter can convert the physical signal. DP++ allows the GPU to output an HDMI-compatible signal directly through the DisplayPort connector, which carries up to 8-channel LPCM.
  • Active Adapters: If the source port does not support DP++, or if you need higher video bandwidths (such as 4K at 120Hz via HDMI 2.1), an active adapter is required. High-quality active adapters contain dedicated converter chips that translate the DisplayPort audio and video stream into an HDMI stream while preserving the multi-channel LPCM packets.

Hardware and Driver Requirements

Modern dedicated graphics cards from NVIDIA and AMD, as well as integrated graphics from Intel, include integrated high-definition audio controllers capable of outputting 8-channel LPCM over DisplayPort.

When connected, the source device reads the Extended Display Identification Data (EDID) from the AV receiver. This handshake informs the graphics card of the receiver’s audio capabilities, including supported channel counts (e.g., 2.0, 5.1, or 7.1) and supported sample rates. If the adapter or cable interrupts this handshake, the system may fall back to standard 2-channel stereo.

Operating System Configuration

Unlike bitstreamed compressed formats (such as Dolby Atmos or DTS:X) that are decoded directly by the receiver, multi-channel LPCM requires the host operating system or playback software to decode the audio tracks first.

To ensure the signal reaches the receiver properly:

  1. Open the sound settings of the operating system (such as the Windows Sound Control Panel).
  2. Select the DisplayPort audio device representing your AVR.
  3. Configure the speaker setup manually to "5.1 Surround" or "7.1 Surround" rather than "Stereo."
  4. Set the default format to 24-bit, 48000 Hz or higher.

Once configured, any multi-channel audio decoded by games, media players, or editing software will travel over the DisplayPort connection as pristine, raw multi-channel LPCM to your AV receiver.
