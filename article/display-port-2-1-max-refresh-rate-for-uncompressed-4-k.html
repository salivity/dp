DisplayPort 2.1 Max Refresh Rate for Uncompressed 4K

DisplayPort 2.1 provides sufficient bandwidth to drive a 4K resolution (3840 × 2160) display at a native, uncompressed refresh rate of 240 Hz using 10-bit color (HDR), with theoretical ceilings reaching up to 360 Hz at 8-bit color (SDR). By leveraging the standard's highest transmission profile—UHBR20—monitors can achieve these ultra-fast refresh rates without relying on Display Stream Compression (DSC) or chroma subsampling.

Bandwidth and Transmission Limits

DisplayPort 2.1 achieves these refresh rates using the Ultra-High Bit Rate (UHBR) transmission modes. The maximum configuration, UHBR20, delivers a raw bandwidth of 80 Gbps across four lanes.

After accounting for 128b/132b channel encoding efficiency, UHBR20 yields a maximum effective payload bandwidth of 77.37 Gbps. This substantial data throughput eliminates the transmission bottlenecks that previously limited DisplayPort 1.4 to 4K at 120 Hz without compression.

Uncompressed 4K Refresh Rates by Color Depth

The maximum uncompressed refresh rate depends directly on color depth and blanking intervals (typically calculated using the VESA CVT-RBv2 standard):

  • 10-bit Color (HDR, 30 bpp, 4:4:4): The highest official native refresh rate is 240 Hz. At 3840 × 2160, a 240 Hz signal requires approximately 68 Gbps to 71 Gbps of data throughput depending on blanking overhead, fitting comfortably within UHBR20’s 77.37 Gbps ceiling.
  • 8-bit Color (SDR, 24 bpp, 4:4:4): The mathematical upper limit over UHBR20 is roughly 360 Hz. Transmitting 3840 × 2160 at 360 Hz requires approximately 75.2 Gbps using tight reduced-blanking timings. While 240 Hz is the standard commercial deployment, 360 Hz represents the physical limit of the uncompressed pipeline.

Requirements for Achieving Maximum Uncompressed Speeds

To operate an uncompressed 4K monitor at 240 Hz or higher, the entire hardware chain must support the full DisplayPort 2.1 standard:

  1. UHBR20 Support: Both the host GPU and the display scaler must support the full 20 Gbps per lane specification. Mid-tier DP 2.1 modes, such as UHBR10 (38.69 Gbps effective) and UHBR13.5 (52.22 Gbps effective), top out at 120 Hz and 144 Hz respectively for uncompressed 10-bit 4K.
  2. DP80-Certified Cabling: Achieving the full 77.37 Gbps data rate requires DP80-certified cables, which are tested to handle the signal integrity requirements of 20 Gbps per lane.
  3. Full RGB / 4:4:4 Sampling: The monitor and source must be configured to full uncompressed RGB, ensuring no chroma subsampling (such as 4:2:2 or 4:2:0) is introduced to artificially lower the bandwidth footprint.
