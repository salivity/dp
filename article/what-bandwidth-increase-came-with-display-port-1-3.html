What Bandwidth Increase Came With DisplayPort 1.3?

The introduction of DisplayPort 1.3 marked a major milestone in display interface technology by significantly expanding data transfer capacity. By increasing the raw maximum link rate from 21.6 Gbps to 32.4 Gbps, DisplayPort 1.3 delivered an overall bandwidth increase of 50 percent compared to the previous DisplayPort 1.2 standard. This upgrade enabled higher display resolutions, faster refresh rates, and multi-monitor configurations over a single cable without visual degradation.

The Bandwidth Leap: Comparing DisplayPort 1.2 and 1.3

To understand the scale of the increase, it helps to look at the link architectures of both standards. DisplayPort transmits data across four main lanes.

  • DisplayPort 1.2 (HBR2): Utilized High Bit Rate 2 mode, running at 5.4 Gbps per lane. Across four lanes, this yielded a total raw bandwidth of 21.6 Gbps. After accounting for 8b/10b encoding overhead, the maximum usable data rate was 17.28 Gbps.
  • DisplayPort 1.3 (HBR3): Introduced High Bit Rate 3 mode, elevating the transfer rate to 8.1 Gbps per lane. Across all four lanes, the total raw bandwidth reached 32.4 Gbps. Applying the standard 8b/10b encoding leaves an effective payload data rate of 25.92 Gbps.

The transition from 21.6 Gbps to 32.4 Gbps raw bandwidth—and from 17.28 Gbps to 25.92 Gbps effective bandwidth—represents an exact 50 percent increase in throughput.

What the Added Bandwidth Enabled

Before DisplayPort 1.3, driving single displays beyond 4K resolution at 60 Hz required multi-cable configurations or chroma subsampling. The additional headroom provided by HBR3 solved several key bottlenecks:

  • Uncompressed 4K and 5K Video: A single DisplayPort 1.3 connection could comfortably drive uncompressed 4K UHD (3840 x 2160) at 120 Hz with 24-bit RGB color, or a 5K display (5120 x 2880) at 60 Hz across a single stream.
  • 8K Capability: The specification laid the groundwork for driving an 8K UHD display (7680 x 4320) at 60 Hz using 4:2:0 subsampled pixel format.
  • Multi-Stream Transport (MST) Enhancements: The increased ceiling allowed users to daisy-chain two 4K displays operating at 60 Hz or up to four full HD 1080p monitors simultaneously over a single port.
  • Protocol Encapsulation Support: DisplayPort 1.3 also introduced mandatory support for Dual-Mode (DP++) HDMI 2.0 adapters with HDCP 2.2 content protection, facilitating conversion to modern television standards.

By raising the raw ceiling to 32.4 Gbps, DisplayPort 1.3 provided the bandwidth headroom necessary to transition desktop computing and professional video workflows into the high-refresh 4K and multi-screen era.
