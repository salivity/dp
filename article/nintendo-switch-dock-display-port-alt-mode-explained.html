Nintendo Switch Dock DisplayPort Alt Mode Explained

The Nintendo Switch utilizes USB Type-C DisplayPort Alternate Mode (DP Alt Mode) to output video from the console to an external display through its dock. Rather than routing native HDMI from the handheld, the Switch transmits raw DisplayPort audio and video data over reconfigured USB-C data lanes, which the dock actively converts to HDMI. This process relies on a strict USB Power Delivery handshake, dynamic pin remapping, and internal hardware signal conversion.

The USB-PD Handshake and Mode Negotiation

When the Switch is inserted into the dock, the two devices establish a connection via the USB-C Configuration Channel (CC) lines. Communication begins using the USB Power Delivery (USB-PD) protocol. The dock acts as a specialized power intermediary and accessory.

Before any video signal is transmitted, the dock's onboard microcontroller exchanges Vendor Defined Messages (VDMs) with the Switch's power management and USB controllers. The dock verifies that a compliant power source is connected—specifically requesting the official 15V power profile. Once power parameters and device identities are mutually authenticated, the Switch boosts its internal clock speeds to "docked mode" and the system triggers the command to enter DisplayPort Alt Mode.

Pin Assignment and Lane Allocation

In standard USB-C operation, high-speed differential lines carry USB 3.x data. Upon entering DP Alt Mode, the Switch switches the function of these physical pins:

  • Video Lanes: Two high-speed differential pairs (TX/RX) are remapped to carry two lanes of DisplayPort 1.2 signaling. With HBR2 (High Bit Rate 2) speeds, two lanes provide up to 10.8 Gbps of raw bandwidth, which is more than sufficient for the console's maximum target output of 1080p at 60 Hz with 8-bit color and multichannel PCM audio.
  • Sideband Channels: The USB-C Sideband Use (SBU) pins are reassigned to function as the DisplayPort Auxiliary (AUX) channel. This bidirectional link handles link management, Extended Display Identification Data (EDID) handshakes, and HDCP encryption between the Switch and the dock.
  • Standard Data: The remaining pins continue handling USB 2.0 communication, maintaining connectivity for the dock's external USB ports and wired LAN adapters without interfering with the video stream.

Internal DisplayPort to HDMI Conversion

The Nintendo Switch does not natively generate an HDMI signal. Televisions overwhelmingly rely on HDMI interfaces, so the dock bridges this gap with an active conversion chipset.

In the standard dock, the Switch routes its 2-lane DisplayPort 1.2 signal directly into an internal Megachips STDP2550 Mobility DisplayPort (MyDP) to HDMI bridge chip (updated to a Realtek RTD2172N converter in the OLED dock model). This integrated circuit demultiplexes the incoming DisplayPort data, decodes the stream, and re-encodes it into standard HDMI signaling. The converted signal is then routed directly to the dock's female HDMI output port, allowing the user to connect to any standard TV or monitor.
