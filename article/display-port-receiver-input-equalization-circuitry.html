DisplayPort Receiver Input Equalization Circuitry

DisplayPort receiver silicon relies on sophisticated input equalization circuitry to counteract signal degradation caused by high-frequency attenuation, impedance mismatches, and inter-symbol interference (ISI) across physical transmission media. As DisplayPort data rates have scaled from legacy RBR/HBR modes up to ultra-high bit rates (UHBR 10, 13.5, and 20 Gbps per lane), receiver architectures have evolved to combine both linear and non-linear equalization techniques. The most common circuits integrated into modern DisplayPort receiver physical layers (PHY) include Variable Gain Amplifiers (VGA), Continuous-Time Linear Equalizers (CTLE), and Decision Feedback Equalizers (DFE).

Variable Gain Amplifier (VGA)

Before deep filtering occurs, the incoming differential signal typically passes through an analog front-end stage featuring a Variable Gain Amplifier or Automatic Gain Control (AGC) circuit. Because copper cables attenuate signal amplitude over varying lengths, the VGA provides adjustable flat-band gain. This stage normalizes the dynamic range of the incoming signal to prevent receiver saturation while ensuring sufficient amplitude for subsequent equalization stages and data slicers.

Continuous-Time Linear Equalizer (CTLE)

The CTLE is a primary linear equalization circuit integrated directly at the receiver input. High-speed signals transmitted over printed circuit board (PCB) traces and DisplayPort cables experience frequency-dependent loss, where high-frequency components suffer significantly higher attenuation than low-frequency components.

A CTLE acts as an active high-pass filter with an adjustable frequency response. By providing peaking at high frequencies relative to lower frequencies, it effectively inverts the channel’s low-pass transfer function. DisplayPort receivers implement multi-stage, programmable CTLE designs that allow the silicon to tune zero and pole locations during the link training sequence, thereby opening the closed data eye at the sampling point.

Decision Feedback Equalizer (DFE)

While a CTLE effectively compensates for insertion loss, relying solely on linear amplification can boost high-frequency crosstalk and channel noise. To mitigate this, DisplayPort receiver silicon integrates a non-linear Decision Feedback Equalizer.

A DFE functions by subtracting the weighted history of previously resolved bits from the current incoming symbol. Because it operates on hard decisions rather than the continuous analog waveform, it cancels post-cursor ISI without amplifying high-frequency noise. In modern DisplayPort standards (such as DP 2.1), multi-tap DFEs are standard, allowing the receiver to handle reflections and multi-symbol ISI induced by connectors and cable imperfections.

Adaptation Logic and Link Training

These analog equalization blocks are governed by digital adaptation engines, typically utilizing Sign-Sign Least Mean Squares (SS-LMS) algorithms. During the DisplayPort Link Training process, the receiver evaluates the incoming signal quality via internal error-detection circuitry and eye-monitoring circuits. The receiver automatically adapts its internal CTLE peaking and DFE tap coefficients while simultaneously using the DisplayPort AUX channel to request changes in the transmitter's pre-emphasis and voltage swing settings. Together, this hybrid architecture ensures robust signal integrity across diverse cable lengths and interconnect designs.
