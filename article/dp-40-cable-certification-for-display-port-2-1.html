DP40 Cable Certification for DisplayPort 2.1

DP40 cable certification is a performance standard established by VESA (Video Electronics Standards Association) confirming that a cable can reliably transmit data at speeds up to 40 gigabits per second (Gbps) under the DisplayPort 2.1 specification. This certification guarantees that modern graphics cards and high-resolution monitors can communicate without signal degradation, frame drops, or visual artifacts. This guide breaks down what the DP40 rating signifies for display bandwidth, resolution support, hardware compatibility, and everyday cable reliability.

Bandwidth and Transmission Speeds

The "40" in DP40 directly represents the maximum total link bandwidth of 40 Gbps. Under the DisplayPort 2.1 standard, this is achieved using the UHBR10 (Ultra-High Bit Rate 10) signaling rate across four high-speed lanes, with each lane carrying 10 Gbps. DisplayPort 2.1 uses a highly efficient 128b/132b channel encoding scheme, resulting in an effective maximum data throughput of approximately 38.69 Gbps—significantly higher than the 25.92 Gbps maximum payload of DisplayPort 1.4a.

Supported Resolutions and Refresh Rates

For displays and graphics processing units (GPUs), a certified DP40 cable provides sufficient uncompressed bandwidth to drive high-end visual configurations:

  • Uncompressed 4K: Native 4K (3840x2160) at up to 144Hz with 10-bit HDR color.
  • Higher Refresh 4K: Native 4K at up to 240Hz using VESA Display Stream Compression (DSC).
  • 8K Content: 8K (7680x4320) at 60Hz when utilizing DSC.
  • Ultrawide Formats: High-refresh ultrawide resolutions (such as 3440x1440 or 5120x1440) at fluid framerates without visual compromise.

Hardware Compatibility and Interoperability

A DP40 certification indicates seamless interoperability between certified DisplayPort 2.1 source devices (such as AMD Radeon RX 7000 series desktop and workstation GPUs) and DP 2.1 displays.

Because DisplayPort standards are backward-compatible, a DP40 cable functions reliably with legacy DisplayPort 1.4 and 1.2 ports, although speeds will automatically step down to match the older hardware. Additionally, the DP40 standard applies to both full-sized DisplayPort connectors and Mini DisplayPort, as well as native DisplayPort over USB-C (DP Alt Mode) connections.

DP40 vs. DP80 Certification

While DP40 handles up to 40 Gbps, VESA also offers a higher tier known as DP80, which supports UHBR20 (80 Gbps). The main distinctions between the two include:

  • Target Hardware: Most current consumer DisplayPort 2.1 monitors and graphics cards utilize UHBR10 or UHBR13.5 connections, making DP40 cables optimal for mainstream and gaming setups. DP80 is primarily designed for niche, high-tier workstation applications.
  • Cable Length: DP40 cables maintain signal stability at longer passive lengths—typically up to 2 meters (approximately 6.6 feet). In contrast, full-bandwidth passive DP80 cables are generally limited to around 1 meter (3.3 feet) to prevent signal loss.

Guaranteed Signal Integrity

The DP40 certification mark assures users that the physical cable has passed rigorous testing for electromagnetic interference (EMI) resistance, return loss, and crosstalk. In high-performance gaming or production environments, using a certified DP40 cable eliminates common digital connection failures, such as intermittent black screens, flickering, and handshake errors between the graphics card and the monitor.
