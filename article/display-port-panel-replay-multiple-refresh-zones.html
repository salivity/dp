DisplayPort Panel Replay Multiple Refresh Zones

DisplayPort Panel Replay optimizes transmission bandwidth and system power by allowing the host GPU to transmit only the modified regions of a display frame rather than redrawing the entire screen. However, DisplayPort Panel Replay cannot synchronize multiple, fully independent refresh rate zones across a single display panel. While it facilitates granular selective updates, the underlying display hardware and interface standard still adhere to a single global refresh timing cycle for the entire screen.

How Panel Replay Operates

Introduced to standard DisplayPort environments from embedded DisplayPort (eDP) Panel Self Refresh (PSR2) technology, Panel Replay relies on a dedicated frame buffer integrated into the sink's Timing Controller (TCON). When content on the display remains static, the host enters a lower-power state, and the display self-refreshes from its internal memory.

When changes occur, Panel Replay uses Selective Update. The GPU identifies the precise rectangular coordinates where pixel data has changed—such as a blinking text cursor or a playing video window—and transmits only those coordinate blocks across the main link.

Selective Update vs. Independent Refresh Zones

There is a fundamental technical distinction between selective frame updating and independent refresh zone synchronization:

  • Selective Update: Transmits spatial sub-regions of pixel data over the link. While only the changed area consumes interface bandwidth, those pixels are committed to the panel's unified frame buffer.
  • Independent Refresh Zones: Requires different physical areas of the panel to cycle their liquid crystals (or OLED subpixels) at entirely different frequencies simultaneously—such as rendering a 30 Hz video window in one quadrant while a 144 Hz application runs smoothly in another without frame tearing or stutter.

Panel Replay operates at the transmission and buffer level, not at the physical panel scanout layer. The TCON still scans lines sequentially across the entire active display area at a single operational frequency determined by the current refresh timing or Variable Refresh Rate (VRR) state.

Hardware and Timing Constraints

A standard display panel features row and column drivers governed by a single master timing generator. Driving distinct spatial zones at genuinely asynchronous refresh rates would require:

  1. Segmented Driver Architecture: Electrically isolated gate-line drivers capable of addressing separate screen sections independently.
  2. Asynchronous TCON Processing: Multiple independent scanout pipelines within the panel silicon to manage distinct vertical blanking intervals (VBI) for each zone.
  3. Artifact Prevention: Mitigating visual artifacts at the boundaries where two independently refreshing display zones meet, which frequently causes luminance mismatch and inversion flicker in liquid crystal matrices.

DisplayPort Panel Replay does not define, coordinate, or synchronize asynchronous multi-zone vertical blanking intervals. Any attempt to update regions at disparate framerates must still resolve to the single, shared vertical scan rate of the target display.

Conclusion

DisplayPort Panel Replay reduces link bandwidth and system power consumption through selective regional updates, but it cannot synchronize multiple independent refresh rate zones on a single panel. Screen updates remain bound to a single unified scanout frequency managed globally by the display panel’s timing controller.
