DisplayPort 2.1 Over Long USB4 Passive Cables

DisplayPort 2.1 introduces tighter electrical convergence with the USB4 standard, enabling ultra-high bit rate (UHBR) video signals to run reliably across longer USB4 passive cables. When transmitting over these extended passive copper connections, the standard actively compensates for high insertion loss and signal degradation by leveraging USB-C E-marker cable discovery, advanced adaptive link training, dynamic equalization adjustments, bit rate step-downs, and mandatory Forward Error Correction (FEC).

Cable Identification via E-Marker Detection

Before high-speed transmission begins, the DisplayPort source interrogates the USB4 passive cable’s electronically marked chip (E-marker) via the USB Power Delivery (PD) channel. This process identifies the cable's physical properties, rated bandwidth, and channel attenuation characteristics. By determining the physical limits of the passive wire beforehand, the DisplayPort 2.1 transmitter establishes a baseline for maximum safe transmission speeds (such as DP40 or DP80 specifications) and configures its initial signal drive levels accordingly.

Adaptive Link Training and Equalization Tuning

Longer passive cables naturally suffer from severe high-frequency attenuation, impedance mismatches, and inter-symbol interference (ISI). DisplayPort 2.1 resolves this through an updated Link Training process that closely mirrors the USB4 physical layer (PHY):

  • Transmitter Pre-Emphasis and De-Emphasis: The transmitter dynamically adjusts its Feed-Forward Equalization (FFE) taps. By boosting high frequencies relative to low frequencies at the source, it pre-distorts the transmitted waveform to counteract expected cable losses.
  • Receiver Equalization (CTLE and DFE): The display receiver applies Continuous-Time Linear Equalization (CTLE) and multi-tap Decision Feedback Equalization (DFE) to open the closed signal "eye" resulting from high channel loss over the passive distance.
  • Link Training Tunable PHY Repeaters (LTTPRs): If repeaters or retimers exist within the signal path, DP 2.1 uses LTTPR protocols to fine-tune each segment of the link independently, ensuring that transmitter parameters meet the receiver's target bit error rate (BER).

Dynamic Bit Rate Negotiation (UHBR Tiering)

DisplayPort 2.1 natively supports three Ultra-High Bit Rate profiles: UHBR10 (10 Gbps per lane), UHBR13.5 (13.5 Gbps per lane), and UHBR20 (20 Gbps per lane). Over extended passive USB4 cables (typically exceeding 0.8 to 1 meter), physical signal degradation often prevents UHBR20 from maintaining an open eye.

During the handshake phase, if the transceiver detects that the high-frequency channel loss exceeds the signal-to-noise threshold required for 20 Gbps, the link dynamically shifts down to UHBR13.5 or UHBR10. This fallback mechanism ensures that the maximum possible bandwidth is delivered without causing link instability or packet loss.

Channel Coding and Forward Error Correction

To manage the higher noise floors typical of longer passive runs, DP 2.1 employs 128b/132b channel encoding, replacing the legacy 8b/10b scheme. This encoding provides 96.7% link efficiency while supporting mandatory Forward Error Correction (FEC). FEC enables the receiver to identify and correct bit errors on the fly without requesting retransmissions, guaranteeing uninterrupted video output even when the passive physical medium pushes transmission margins to their limits.
